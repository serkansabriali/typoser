The invisible art of typographic systems

Good type is invisible. Readers don't notice the typeface, the size, or the spacing — they simply move through content without friction. This effect isn't accidental; it's the result of a consistent, considered system applied at every level.

Why systems matter

A typographic system replaces hundreds of micro-decisions with a single source of truth. Every heading, every label, every caption follows a defined relationship. Consistency compounds — the benefit grows as the surface area of a product grows.

Scale and hierarchy

Hierarchy isn't just about making things bigger. Weight, line height, and tracking all contribute. A heading at 28px with generous leading reads clearly as a section break even when its weight matches the body text below it.

Within a section

Sub-headings group related content. At 22px they're clearly a heading — but they don't compete with the section title above them.

The best typographic systems are invisible. They give readers a clear path through content without announcing themselves at every step.

List items follow the same style as body text — reading is continuous whether the content is prose or enumerated.
